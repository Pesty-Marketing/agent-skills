# Pyramid Principle

Organize communication into **main message → supporting points → evidence**. Lead with the recommendation, answer, or conclusion, then develop its support. Apply to the requested message, document, talking points, or presentation outline; an outline does not authorize producing a deck. This skill stands alone. Combining it with SCR is separate work unless requested.

## Establish the message

Identify the audience, purpose, requested format and length, intended conclusion, and available evidence from the supplied context. Ask a focused question only when missing information would materially change the result.

Preserve a supplied recommendation while checking whether the evidence supports it. If the evidence contradicts it, expose the conflict rather than manufacturing a persuasive case or silently substituting a different decision. If no conclusion is supplied, derive one only as strongly as the available evidence permits; identify a material gap when it prevents a sound conclusion.

## Build the pyramid

- **Main message:** Lead with one concise overall point. Favor an actionable recommendation when the task calls for one; use a direct answer or conclusion for informational requests. Replace a chronological buildup with the central point while retaining context necessary to understand it.
- **Supporting points:** Group the reasons for the conclusion or the parts of the proposed plan. Keep sibling points at comparable levels of detail and use a coherent organizing principle within each group. Let substance determine the number: three is a convention, not a quota.
- **Evidence:** Place facts, examples, and concrete rationale under the point they support. Distinguish factual evidence from illustrative examples, assumptions, and reasoning; preserve available source attribution when needed to assess a claim. Missing support is a gap to expose, not a reason to invent data.

Adapt the depth and formatting to the audience, medium, and requested length. The three levels describe the logical structure, not mandatory headings or a requirement to make every message long.

## Check the logic

**Vertical:** Does each piece of evidence support its parent point, and does each point support the main message? Repair missing links, misplaced evidence, and unsupported inferences. A correlation or example alone does not establish causation. Keep material counterarguments and conflicting evidence visible, and explain their implications for the recommendation.

**Horizontal:** Are sibling points distinct, logically ordered, and sufficient to answer the audience's important questions? Look for redundant arguments, mixed categories, and missing parts of the decision or process. Identify consequential missing information rather than fabricating an answer.

Use MECE—mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive—as a practical check on grouping and coverage. Give repeated evidence a primary home; explain genuine interactions or cross-cutting evidence when necessary rather than implying independent categories or counting the same support twice. Completeness means sufficient coverage for this audience and decision, not everything known about the topic.

Qualify conclusions when support is limited. A well-organized argument does not prove its claims true, and passing a structural check does not prove exhaustive coverage.

## Draft and review outputs

**Drafting:** Return one ready-to-use message, then a compact breakdown labeled **Main message**, **Supporting points**, and **Evidence**. Keep the correspondence between points and evidence apparent without reproducing the full message. Follow explicit requests for message-only, outline-only, or a different format.

**Reviewing:** Give a short assessment of consequential weaknesses, an improved version, and the structural breakdown. Distinguish wording and organization changes from proposed changes in substance. For critique-only requests, provide the assessment without a rewrite.

Finish when the message leads with a clear point, its supporting logic is traceable, material gaps and counterarguments remain visible, and the output fits the requested scope. If a gap prevents a usable result, identify it rather than presenting a provisional argument as ready to use.
