# Pesty voice for explainer scripts

Source of truth: the `pesty-design` skill's readme (CONTENT FUNDAMENTALS). This is that voice adapted for narration.

## Who's listening
A pest control operator, usually the owner: runs trucks and techs, knows their market cold, is sceptical of marketing agencies, and measures everything in calls, booked jobs, recurring customers and cost per acquisition. They have $1M+ in revenue and want 15–30%+ growth. Respect their intelligence; never explain pest control to them.

## How Pesty sounds
- **Operator-to-operator.** "You" for the viewer, a confident "we" for Pesty. A partner with skin in the game, not a soft creative agency.
- **Plain-spoken and punchy.** Short declarative sentences. Fragments for emphasis. Concrete nouns: trucks, techs, the map pack, peak season, the phone.
- **Specific, never vague.** Lead with the metric, then the story. "Three hundred twenty-nine new recurring customers" beats "a lot more customers".
- **Confident, bordering on swagger, always backed by numbers.** Recurring metaphors of dominance and celebration ("own your market", "beat your monthly target, then take the team out") are on-brand in small doses: once or twice a video, not every line.
- **Industry shorthand is fine** (PCO, CAC, CPL, LSAs, map pack). Spell out an acronym once if the video teaches it.
- **No hype words without proof** ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "skyrocket"). No emoji, obviously.

## Proof rules
- Every number and named result comes from `sources.md`: the user's materials or pestymarketing.com (re-check the live page; case-study figures can change). Never estimate, round up, or invent a client.
- Name a client only if the source names them publicly. Otherwise "one of our clients" or "a Pesty client in Texas" (only if the source says Texas).
- If no real number supports the point, make the point without one. A fabricated stat in a marketing video can damage Pesty more than a vague line.
- Figures that have appeared on pestymarketing.com (verify before use): $100,000 of monthly ad spend saved; 329 new recurring customers at a $72.04 CAC; $24.24 cost per qualified lead; 100,000+ qualified leads generated; Pesty works with one client per city and with PCOs already past $1M a year.

## Characters
The protagonist is a PCO owner or operator with a name and a concrete business ("Mike runs a nine-truck shop outside Tampa", "Dana took over her dad's termite company"). The competitor down the road, a tech in the field, a homeowner searching at 9pm with a wasp nest: all good supporting cast. Pesty itself rarely appears as a character; it shows up in the proof and the CTA.

## The ending
The takeaway is one sentence an owner would repeat to their office manager. Then the end card: a short promise (≤ 6 words, Epilogue, e.g. "Own your city's map pack.") and one CTA. Pick the CTA by where the video lives:
- Sales / website / YouTube (default): "Book your strategy call", url `pestymarketing.com`.
- Scarcity angle, when the video is about market dominance: "Check if your city's available", url `pestymarketing.com`.
- Pure education with no sales intent: no CTA pill; logo + promise + url.
The narration's last line says the CTA in plain words ("If you want this working in your market, book a call.") rather than a scripted sales pitch.
